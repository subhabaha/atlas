The Ingestion Bench

Everything downstream is capped here. Naive extraction destroys a torque table silently — the numbers survive, the row-to-value relationship does not — and a fixed 512-token boundary cuts a lock-out procedure in half. The answer stays fluent the whole way down.

The scenario

The corpus is real: the US Department of Energy Fundamentals Handbooks — Mechanical Science, DOE-HDBK-1018/1 and 1018/2 (1993). Several hundred public-domain pages on pumps, valves, heat exchangers and diesel engines, with the multi-column layout, torque and specification tables, numbered safety procedures and figure callouts that break naive extraction. You cannot demonstrate an extraction failure on clean text.

We ingest the same manual four ways, ask it the same ten questions, and find the question every method except the best one answers confidently and wrongly. This is the least glamorous stage in the pipeline and it is where the largest single accuracy gain usually lives.

What the attendees must build

  • Four extraction levels: naive (pypdf), layout-aware (pymupdf blocks), table-preserving (pdfplumber → markdown), metadata-rich
  • One table page printed at all four levels, side by side, on the screen
  • The 4×10 grid — forty cells, each correct or incorrect against the recorded gold answer
  • The most instructive failure written up: question, page, extracted text, wrong answer, and why
  • A numbered lock-out procedure demonstrably cut by a fixed 512-token boundary
  • Passage-count comparison at top-3 / 8 / 20 with quality, tokens, cost and latency
  • A token-budget table instrumented with tiktoken, incl. the tool-schema tax at 0/3/12 tools
  • The part-number tax measured: HX-4471-B fragments into 6–9 tokens that carry no meaning

The answer the model returns

        
“To service the unit: shut it down with the normal stop sequence, apply your personal lock and
          tag, bleed the receiver to 0 bar and discharge stored electrical energy, then begin work.” It is fluent, complete-sounding, and
          it describes an unsafe procedure — the energy sources were never isolated and zero-energy was never verified.
          This is the single strongest argument for section-aware chunking.

The trap Copilot will hand you

    

      
A round chunk size, chosen for you

      
Ask Copilot to build the pipeline and it defaults to fixed-size chunking with a round number —
        512, no overlap — because that is what most tutorials do. Fixed size is the baseline you are measuring against,
        not the answer. Its boundaries land wherever the token count says, including the middle of a safety procedure.

    

    

      
Numbers surviving is not extraction working

      
Level-1 text still contains “23 17 55 41 110 83”. It looks extracted. But a question about a
        specific bolt returns a passage with the right number somewhere among five others, and the model picks one — fluent,
        confident, and wrong roughly four times in five. Verify the relationship, not the presence of digits.

    

    

      
Show what was retrieved, not what was answered

      
Almost every diagnosis today starts with the same prompt: “Print the exact passages passed to
        the model, with their scores and token counts, before the answer.” The cut procedure above is invisible in the answer and
        obvious in the retrieved chunk.

    

  


  
  

    

      
The lesson this build makes undeniable

      
Ingestion quality caps everything downstream.

      
No retrieval strategy finds a table that extraction flattened; no reranker rescues a procedure
        that chunking cut in half; no prompt makes the answer right. The largest single accuracy gain in the whole pipeline is
        usually the least glamorous change — moving from level 1 to level 3, measured as +6 questions of 10.

    

    

      
Defend your result

      
“Level 1 extraction answered 3 of 10; level 3
        answered 9. Our worst failure was the 5/8-11 lubricated torque, and it happened because naive text collapsed the
        table to a run-on and the model picked the wrong number. Fixed 512-token chunking cut the lock-out procedure in half.
        Top 20 cost 3× top 3 and scored 9 points lower.”
