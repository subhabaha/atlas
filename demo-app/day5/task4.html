The Capstone

Assembly, not new work. Sixteen of the eighteen items already exist in your assignments from Monday to Thursday. A roadmap with an honest ceiling — 0.61 to an estimated 0.78–0.84, ceiling 0.88 — is more credible than a promise of 95%, and it is the version that survives the first month of contact with the data.

What this is, and what it is not

The brief that started this programme asked for a route to 95%. This is where the week answers it — credibly. Teams that treat the capstone as new work panic and produce worse capstones. Where an item does not exist yet, mark it as a gap with a plan — that is a better answer than inventing something, and it is scored as such.

You are judged on diagnostic honesty, architectural soundness, measurement rigour and realism of the plan — not on how good the numbers look. Naming an uncomfortable finding makes you more credible, not less.

Name the biggest risk — the thing most likely to make this not work
Biggest risk · label disagreement is structural, not fixable by more data

Two defect classes overlap in the current definitions, so annotators disagree 12% of the time. Every model we train inherits that ceiling. If the definitions are not renegotiated with the people who own the taxonomy, no amount of retrieval or calibration moves us past 0.88 — and month-two model work will look like it failed when the real limit is the label.

The trap the capstone sets

Promising 95%

The brief asked for 95%, so the tempting slide promises 95%. But the task's own label agreement caps the achievable accuracy at 0.88. A point estimate above the ceiling is not ambition, it is a claim the data cannot support — and it does not survive the first month of contact with production failures.

A projection stated as a point

"We will reach 0.81." Reach it how surely? A point hides the uncertainty that a range makes honest. State 0.78–0.84, and carry the evidence behind each delta so the range is defensible rather than decorative.

Model work in the first thirty days

You will feel pressure from your own management to show model progress in month one. Have the argument ready: a plan that starts with measurement is the plan that survives contact with the data. The first thirty days are the evaluation set, the taxonomy, the leakage checklist and the baseline — not a new model.

The lesson this build makes undeniable

A roadmap with an honest ceiling is more credible than a promise of 95%.

The capstone is assembly: sixteen of eighteen items already exist. What makes it credible is not the height of the number but the honesty of the interval — a measured baseline, evidence-backed deltas, a range not a point, a ceiling named with its evidence, and a plan that measures before it models.

The closing verdict

“These five interventions take us from 0.61 to an estimated 0.78–0.84; the ceiling is 0.88 given current label agreement; and closing the remainder to 0.95 requires redefining two defect classes — a labelling problem, not a modelling one. That is far more valuable, and far more credible, than a promise of 95%.”
