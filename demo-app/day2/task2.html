The Nineteen Layers

A proof of concept has three components: a script, a model call, and a notebook someone runs. A production system has nineteen — and the other sixteen each exist because something failed without them. The facilitator breaks your dependencies while the service runs, and you find out which layers you actually built.

The scenario

You have a working PoC. It answers questions correctly and it cannot be deployed — and the gap between those two facts is sixteen layers. Build the naive version deliberately, measure it, then add the layers that make it a system.

Then the chaos drill: a fixed load of 200 requests through your service while four faults are injected in turn. No code changes during the drill. The number that wins the lab is percentage of requests answered usefully while a dependency is down — and the naive PoC scores zero on every fault.

What the attendees must build

  • A deliberately naive poc.py — no auth, timeout, retry, logging or versioning — measured as the baseline
  • A layers.yaml covering all layers as present / planned / excluded, every exclusion carrying a reason
  • Orchestration with all six responsibilities: routing, sequencing, retry-with-backoff, timeouts, context assembly, guardrail hook
  • Model serving that returns a model version, a prompt-keyed cache, and cheap/expensive routes
  • Prompt and model registries — versioned, authoritative, reported on every response
  • Monitoring, logging and audit as three separate stores with different retention
  • A chaos drill measuring served / failed / p95 / caller-experience for four faults, against both services
  • A scalability risk table naming what breaks at 10× volume and which component first — measured, not guessed

The planted defect the drill finds

Retry without a timeout makes an outage three times longer, not shorter. Copilot gives you retries; it will not give you timeouts. Each attempt waits indefinitely and you have now queued three of them — the slow-endpoint fault turns a 3-second call into a 27-second one. Every external call needs a deadline shorter than your own caller's patience. Grep for it in front of the room.

The lesson this build makes undeniable

The sixteen extra layers are not bureaucracy — each one is a failure you have not had yet.

A PoC that answers correctly and a system that stays up under a broken dependency are different artefacts. The manifest forces a deliberate decision about every layer — including the decision to leave one out, with a reason. The chaos drill converts that decision into a number: percentage answered usefully while a dependency is down.

Defend your result

“Our PoC served 0% of requests during a dependency outage; our layered service served 89%. The layer that made the largest difference was timeouts inside orchestration. We deliberately excluded the graph store and the skill registry because we have no relationship queries and only one skill. At ten times volume the first thing to break is model serving, at 3.2×, and we know because we ran it.”
