Day 2 · Task 3 Lab 3 MODULE 3 · ELEVEN STORAGE LAYERS · DATA CONTRACTS · LINEAGE · SUPERSESSION · EMBEDDING VERSIONS

Ingestion Architecture

Two embedding-model versions in one index degrade retrieval silently — nothing errors, no alert fires. Both models output 1,536 dimensions, so mixing them raises no exception at all. Retrieval simply gets worse, and diagnosing it months later is genuinely hard.

The scenario

Three kinds of data arrive by three routes — technical documents, telemetry, feedback — and each needs a different answer to the same four questions: where does it live, how is it versioned, who may see it, how long is it kept? You build it as a working pipeline, not a diagram, and in the middle you meet the most expensive silent failure in enterprise retrieval.

The corpus: 300 synthetic service bulletins and procedures for the GA-30, GA-75 and ZR-160 families, with a 40-question gold set naming the document that answers each. Retrieval quality must be measurable against known-correct answers.

What the attendees must build

  • All eleven storage layers as separate locations, each with its key property stated
  • A JSON-Schema data contract per data type, enforced at ingestion, that fails loudly
  • At least five of eight quality checks: completeness, validity, consistency, uniqueness, freshness
  • A deliberately corrupted batch rejected with a message naming the violated contract
  • Recall@5 plotted against the proportion of mixed-version content, with the fix applied and recovery shown
  • embedding_model_version recorded on every vector; a model change budgeted as a re-index
  • Supersession latency measured, and the freshness contract stated
  • One earlier answer reproduced exactly, from the lineage record alone

The eleven storage layers — as real directories

A layer you cannot point at does not exist. Pick any object the pipeline creates and name which of the eleven it belongs to; if you cannot, it is probably in three of them inconsistently — and one of the three is somebody's laptop.

An AI system without this absorbs the malformed input, produces a confident wrong answer, and nobody notices for weeks. A rejected batch is a bad morning; a month of quietly degraded answers is a bad quarter.

The centrepiece — the embedding-version experiment

Why it is dangerous

An embedding is only meaningful relative to the model that produced it. Two models with the same output dimensions produce vectors in different spaces; distances between them are meaningless. No exception is raised. The fix: record embedding_model_version on every vector, then split into per-version indexes or re-embed everything with one model — restoring Recall@5 to the 0.90 baseline at a re-index cost of 400 documents · ~12 minutes.

The freshness contract

“For service manuals, nightly batch is fine. For a procedure just withdrawn by a safety notice, it is not — because a system confidently citing a withdrawn procedure is considerably worse than one that finds nothing at all.” Safety notices route through event-driven ingestion; the rest batch overnight.

The lesson this build makes undeniable

Silent degradation is worse than a crash, because nobody is paged for it.

A traditional pipeline fails loudly when a schema changes; an AI system absorbs the malformed input and answers confidently wrong. Validation at ingestion, a model version on every vector, and a lineage record are the three controls that turn silent quality failures into loud, catchable ones — and the embedding-version experiment shows exactly what silence costs.

Defend your result

“Mixing two embedding versions cost us 0.22 points of Recall@5 at 50% mixed content, with no error and no alert. We detected it by re-running the gold set against the proportion of v2 content, and fixed it by tagging every vector with its embedding_model_version and re-embedding — a re-index of 400 docs in ~12 minutes. Our freshness contract for safety notices is event-driven ingestion, which rules out nightly batch.”
